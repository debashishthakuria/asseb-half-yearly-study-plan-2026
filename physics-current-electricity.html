Current Electricity

Drift, Ohm’s law, resistivity, cells, Kirchhoff rules and Wheatstone bridge.

NCERT Physics Part I · chapter 3 · PDF pages 97–135

ConceptFormulasDerivationExampleQuestions

Understand the idea

Electric current is a rate of charge flow. Resistivity is a material property; resistance also depends on length and cross-section. Kirchhoff’s rules conserve charge at a junction and energy around a loop.

Formulas · know when to use them

Current and driftI = neAv_d; J = I/An: carrier density, e: charge magnitude, A: cross-section.
ResistanceV = IR; R = ρL/AFor a metal near a reference temperature: R ≈ R₀[1 + αΔT].
PowerP = VI = I²R = V²/RDistinguish heat in a resistor from total battery power.
Cell with internal resistanceV_terminal = ε − Ir (discharging)I = ε/(R+r) for one external resistance R.
Wheatstone balanceP/Q = R/SAt balance the galvanometer current is zero.

Derivation · Microscopic Ohm’s law

An electron with mean time τ between collisions gains mean drift speed magnitude v_d = eEτ/m. Current I = neAv_d = ne²AτE/m. Since E = V/L, I = (ne²τA/mL)V. Therefore R = mL/(ne²τA), so ρ = m/(ne²τ). State the simple Drude-model assumptions.

Worked example · Cell and load

A 12 V cell with internal resistance 1 Ω is connected to a 5 Ω resistor. Find I and terminal voltage.

I = 12/(5+1) = 2 A. Terminal voltage = 12 − 2(1) = 10 V; also IR = 10 V.

Check yourself

Why does a balanced Wheatstone bridge have no galvanometer current?
Show answer

Its two junctions are at equal potential.

When is terminal voltage less than emf?
Show answer

When a cell is delivering current through its internal resistance.
