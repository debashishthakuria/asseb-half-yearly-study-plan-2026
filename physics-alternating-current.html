Alternating Current

AC rms, reactance, LCR, resonance, power factor and transformer.

NCERT Physics Part I · chapter 7 · PDF pages 237–272

ConceptFormulasDerivationExampleQuestions

Understand the idea

An alternating source changes sign periodically. Inductors and capacitors affect current through frequency-dependent reactance; in a series LCR circuit the current peaks at resonance.

Formulas · know when to use them

RMS valuesV_rms = V₀/√2; I_rms = I₀/√2Valid for sinusoidal AC.
ReactancesX_L = ωL; X_C = 1/(ωC); ω = 2πfPure L: current lags voltage; pure C: current leads.
Series LCRZ = √[R²+(X_L−X_C)²]; I_rms = V_rms/Ztanφ = (X_L−X_C)/R.
Resonanceω₀ = 1/√(LC); f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)At resonance: X_L=X_C, Z=R.
Power and transformerP_avg = V_rms I_rms cosφ; V_s/V_p = N_s/N_pIdeal transformer only; real losses exist.

Derivation · Series LCR impedance

Take current as reference. Resistor voltage is in phase with current, inductor voltage leads it by 90°, capacitor voltage lags by 90°. The reactive voltages oppose: V_rms² = (I_rmsR)² + [I_rms(X_L−X_C)]². Thus Z = V_rms/I_rms = √[R²+(X_L−X_C)²]. At resonance X_L=X_C, so Z=R.

Worked example · Impedance and current

For a series circuit with R=30 Ω, X_L=50 Ω, X_C=10 Ω and V_rms=100 V, find Z and I_rms.

Z = √(30²+40²) = 50 Ω. I_rms = 100/50 = 2 A.
