Electric Charges & Fields

Charge, Coulomb’s law, electric field, dipole, flux and Gauss’s law.

NCERT Physics Part I · chapter 1 · PDF pages 5–54

ConceptFormulasDerivationExampleQuestions

Understand the idea

Charge is conserved and quantised. The electric field is force per unit positive test charge. Flux counts the field passing through a surface; Gauss’s law relates closed-surface flux to enclosed charge.

Formulas · know when to use them

Coulomb’s lawF = k|q₁q₂|/r²; k = 1/(4πε₀)Force along the line joining point charges.
Electric fieldE = F/q₀; E = k|q|/r²Field of a point charge; direction matters.
Flux and GaussΦ = ∫E·dA; ∮E·dA = Q_enclosed/ε₀For a uniform field on a plane: Φ = EA cosθ; θ is to the surface normal.
Dipolep = q(2a); τ = pE sinθp points from negative to positive charge.
Symmetry resultsE_line = λ/(2πε₀r); E_sheet = σ/(2ε₀)Infinite line and infinite non-conducting sheet.

Derivation · Field of an infinite line via Gauss’s law

Choose a closed coaxial cylinder of radius r and length L. By symmetry E is radial and constant on the curved side; flux through its flat ends is zero. Therefore ∮E·dA = E(2πrL) = λL/ε₀, so E = λ/(2πε₀r). State why this method requires an effectively infinite, uniform line.

Worked example · Two equal charges

Two +2 μC point charges are 0.30 m apart. Find the force magnitude.

F = (9 × 10⁹)(2 × 10⁻⁶)²/(0.30)² = 0.40 N. It is repulsive.

Check yourself

What does a closed Gaussian surface with no enclosed net charge tell you?
Show answer

Its net flux is zero, but the electric field at points on it need not be zero.

Why do field lines never cross?
Show answer

A crossing would give two field directions at one point, which is impossible.
