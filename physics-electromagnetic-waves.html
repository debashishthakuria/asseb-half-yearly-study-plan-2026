Electromagnetic Waves

Displacement current, EM-wave properties and spectrum applications.

Understand the idea

A time-varying electric field contributes to a magnetic field (displacement current). In vacuum an electromagnetic wave has mutually perpendicular electric and magnetic fields, both transverse to propagation.

Formulas · know when to use them

Vacuum speedc = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/sIn vacuum, c = fλ.
Field amplitudesE₀/B₀ = cFields are in phase for a plane wave in vacuum.
Displacement currentI_d = ε₀ dΦ_E/dtImportant between charging capacitor plates.
IntensityI_avg = ½ ε₀c E₀²For a sinusoidal plane wave in vacuum.

Derivation · Why a charging capacitor needs displacement current

Apply Ampère’s law to a loop around a charging wire. A spanning surface cutting the wire encloses conduction current I, while a surface bulging between capacitor plates has no conduction current. Maxwell added I_d = ε₀ dΦ_E/dt. Between ideal plates Φ_E = EA = Q/ε₀, so I_d = dQ/dt = I; both surfaces give the same magnetic field.

Worked example · Wavelength

An EM wave in vacuum has frequency 100 MHz. Find wavelength.

λ = c/f = (3 × 10⁸)/(100 × 10⁶) = 3 m.

Check yourself

What are the directions of E, B and propagation in a plane EM wave?
Show answer

They are mutually perpendicular; the propagation direction follows E × B.

Order radio waves, visible light and X-rays by increasing frequency.
Show answer

Radio waves → visible light → X-rays.
