Understand the idea

A moving charge experiences a magnetic force perpendicular to both velocity and field. A steady current creates a magnetic field; symmetry makes Ampère’s law useful for long straight conductors and solenoids.

Derivation · Field around a long straight wire

Take an Amperian circle of radius r centred on the long wire. Cylindrical symmetry makes B tangential and equal in magnitude everywhere on it. ∮B·dl = B(2πr) = μ₀I, giving B = μ₀I/(2πr). Use the right-hand grip rule for direction.

Worked example · Force on a moving proton

A proton moves at 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.5 T field. Find magnetic-force magnitude.

F = evB = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2 × 10⁶)(0.5) = 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ N. Direction follows q(v × B).

Check yourself

Why does magnetic force not change a charged particle’s speed?
Show answer

It is perpendicular to velocity, so its instantaneous work is zero.

What is the field outside an ideal long solenoid?
Show answer

Approximately zero far from its ends.
