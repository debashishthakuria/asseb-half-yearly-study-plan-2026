Electromagnetic Induction

Magnetic flux, Faraday and Lenz, motional emf, self/mutual induction.

NCERT Physics Part I · chapter 6 · PDF pages 208–236

ConceptFormulasDerivationExampleQuestions

Understand the idea

Changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces emf. Lenz’s law specifies the current direction: the induced effect opposes the change in flux, conserving energy.

Formulas · know when to use them

FluxΦ_B = BA cosθAngle θ is between B and the area normal.
Faraday–Lenzε = −N dΦ_B/dtMagnitude for a uniform change: |ε| = N|ΔΦ|/Δt.
Motional emfε = BℓvStraight rod ℓ moving perpendicular to uniform B.
Self-inductionε_L = −L dI/dt; U_L = ½LI²Inductor opposes changes in current.

Derivation · Motional emf of a rod

A conducting rod of length ℓ moves at speed v perpendicular to a uniform field B. Charges in it feel magnetic force qvB. Charge separation builds an electric force qE; at equilibrium qE = qvB, so E = vB. The end-to-end potential difference is ε = Eℓ = Bℓv. The circuit current requires a closed path.

Worked example · Changing-flux coil

A 100-turn coil has flux per turn changing from 0.020 Wb to 0.005 Wb in 0.10 s. Find average emf magnitude.

|ε| = 100 × |0.005 − 0.020| / 0.10 = 15 V. Direction needs the field orientation and Lenz’s law.

Check yourself

Why is there a minus sign in Faraday’s law?
Show answer

It encodes Lenz’s law: induced emf opposes the flux change.

Can an emf be induced without physically moving the loop?
Show answer

Yes, if the magnetic field or loop orientation/area changes so the flux changes.
