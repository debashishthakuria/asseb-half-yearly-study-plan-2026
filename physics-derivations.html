Derivation workshop

Eight compact step-by-step proofs you can rewrite without looking.

How to earn derivation marks

Write the diagram or geometry, symmetry/assumptions, defining equation, algebra and boxed final result. Do not memorise an unexplained last line. A full-board PYQ is not a guaranteed half-yearly question.

1. Field of an infinite line via Gauss’s law

Choose a closed coaxial cylinder of radius r and length L. By symmetry E is radial and constant on the curved side; flux through its flat ends is zero. Therefore ∮E·dA = E(2πrL) = λL/ε₀, so E = λ/(2πε₀r). State why this method requires an effectively infinite, uniform line.

2. Parallel-plate capacitor

Take two large plates ±Q, area A, separation d. Ignoring edge effects, each sheet contributes σ/(2ε₀), so between plates E = σ/ε₀ = Q/(ε₀A). Potential difference V = Ed = Qd/(ε₀A). Hence C = Q/V = ε₀A/d. With a full dielectric, replace ε₀ by κ ε₀.

3. Microscopic Ohm’s law

An electron with mean time τ between collisions gains mean drift speed magnitude v_d = eEτ/m. Current I = neAv_d = ne²AτE/m. Since E = V/L, I = (ne²τA/mL)V. Therefore R = mL/(ne²τA), so ρ = m/(ne²τ). State the simple Drude-model assumptions.

4. Field around a long straight wire

Take an Amperian circle of radius r centred on the long wire. Cylindrical symmetry makes B tangential and equal in magnitude everywhere on it. ∮B·dl = B(2πr) = μ₀I, giving B = μ₀I/(2πr). Use the right-hand grip rule for direction.

5. Torque on a magnetic dipole

For a small current loop in uniform B, opposite sides experience equal and opposite forces. Their resultant force is zero but they form a couple. The torque is τ = IAB sinθ for one turn, or NIAB sinθ for N turns. Defining m = NIA gives τ = mB sinθ, tending to align m with B.

6. Motional emf of a rod

A conducting rod of length ℓ moves at speed v perpendicular to a uniform field B. Charges in it feel magnetic force qvB. Charge separation builds an electric force qE; at equilibrium qE = qvB, so E = vB. The end-to-end potential difference is ε = Eℓ = Bℓv. The circuit current requires a closed path.

7. Series LCR impedance

Take current as reference. Resistor voltage is in phase with current, inductor voltage leads it by 90°, capacitor voltage lags by 90°. The reactive voltages oppose: V_rms² = (I_rmsR)² + [I_rms(X_L−X_C)]². Thus Z = V_rms/I_rms = √[R²+(X_L−X_C)²]. At resonance X_L=X_C, so Z=R.

8. Why a charging capacitor needs displacement current

Apply Ampère’s law to a loop around a charging wire. A spanning surface cutting the wire encloses conduction current I, while a surface bulging between capacitor plates has no conduction current. Maxwell added I_d = ε₀ dΦ_E/dt. Between ideal plates Φ_E = EA = Q/ε₀, so I_d = dQ/dt = I; both surfaces give the same magnetic field.
