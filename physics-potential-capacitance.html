Potential & Capacitance

Potential, equipotentials, dipole energy, capacitors, dielectrics and stored energy.

NCERT Physics Part I · chapter 2 · PDF pages 55–96

ConceptFormulasDerivationExampleQuestions

Understand the idea

Potential is electric potential energy per unit charge. Electric field points towards decreasing potential. Capacitance measures charge stored per volt; the dielectric changes capacitance and may change charge or voltage depending on what is held fixed.

Formulas · know when to use them

PotentialV = kq/r; ΔU = qΔVPotential is a scalar; take care with charge signs.
Field–potentialE = −dV/dr (along r)Use the negative gradient in the field direction.
CapacitanceC = Q/V; C_parallel plate = ε₀A/dWith fully filled dielectric: C = κ ε₀A/d.
NetworksC_parallel = ΣCᵢ; 1/C_series = Σ1/CᵢParallel: same V. Series: same charge magnitude.
EnergyU = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = ½QVUse the correct constraints after disconnecting a battery.

Derivation · Parallel-plate capacitor

Take two large plates ±Q, area A, separation d. Ignoring edge effects, each sheet contributes σ/(2ε₀), so between plates E = σ/ε₀ = Q/(ε₀A). Potential difference V = Ed = Qd/(ε₀A). Hence C = Q/V = ε₀A/d. With a full dielectric, replace ε₀ by κ ε₀.

Worked example · Series capacitors

A 2 μF and a 3 μF capacitor are in series across 10 V. Find Q and the voltage across 2 μF.

C_eq = (2×3)/(2+3) = 1.2 μF; Q = C_eqV = 12 μC. V₂ = Q/C₂ = 6 V (and V₃ = 4 V).

Check yourself

Can an equipotential surface cross an electric field line at an oblique angle?
Show answer

No. The field is perpendicular to an equipotential surface.

What stays constant for a capacitor disconnected from its battery?
Show answer

Charge Q (assuming no leakage); voltage can change.
